Moving to Italy

Moving to Italy

Legal Assistance for International Residents

Relocating

Residence, home, wealth and family, together.

Moving to Italy means organising residence, home, wealth and family at the same time. The firm handles the legal side of the move and coordinates the other professionals involved.

Meetings are held in English or Italian, in person or by video call.

Typical steps

  1. 01

    Preliminary assessment

    An initial meeting to understand your objectives, timing, family situation and the interests you plan to bring to Italy, and to identify the legal issues to address.

  2. 02

    Residence and tax coordination

    The legal framework of your change of residence and, where required, checking entry and residence permits with the competent professionals and authorities. Tax planning, including any election for the new-residents regime, is carried out by tax advisers with whom the firm coordinates.

  3. 03

    Property and due diligence

    Checks on the property (title, planning compliance, encumbrances), review of the offer and preliminary contract, and assistance through to completion before the notary, including purchases through a company.

  4. 04

    Structuring your assets

    How to hold assets, shareholdings and investments in Italy, with input from the necessary specialists.

  5. 05

    Family and succession

    Family and succession matters with international elements: habitual residence in Italy may affect the law applicable to succession (EU Regulation No 650/2012), with consequences for wills and estate planning.

The first meeting

What to prepare

No documents are needed for the first meeting: any papers will be requested later, through confidential channels.

  • Current country of residence and citizenship
  • Indicative timing of the move and preferred locations
  • Who is moving: an individual, a couple or a family
  • The general nature of the interests you plan to bring (property, shareholdings, business activities)
  • Advisers already involved in your home country
  • The questions that matter most to you

What the firm does not do

Specialist tax advice (planning, tax returns, elections for preferential regimes) is provided by accountants and tax advisers with whom the firm coordinates.

Matters governed by the law of your home country are handled by lawyers qualified in that jurisdiction. The firm has no offices or professional qualifications outside Italy.
